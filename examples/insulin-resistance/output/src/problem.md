Concept to learn: insulin resistance, and why South Asians face higher
risk of diabetes, hypertension, and heart disease, at a given body size,
compared to other population groups.

Problem:
- Insulin normally lets cells take in glucose from the blood.
- Insulin resistance means cells respond less to insulin. The body makes
  more insulin to compensate. Over time, blood glucose rises regardless.
- Standard risk cutoffs, for example body mass index (BMI) of 30 for
  obesity, were set from mostly European study populations.
- South Asians show higher insulin resistance, and higher diabetes risk,
  at a lower BMI, and at a lower waist size, than these standard cutoffs
  suggest.
- South Asians often carry more fat around the organs, and less muscle,
  at a given weight, than other groups. This pattern is sometimes called
  the "thin-fat" body type.
- This pattern raises the risk of type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure,
  and heart disease, often at a younger age, and at a lower weight, than
  current guidelines assume.
